Why giraffes are wildly underrated

Real biology, kept short.

Same vertebrae as you

Despite that enormous neck, a giraffe has exactly seven neck vertebrae — the same number found in humans and nearly every other mammal. Each one is just dramatically longer, some over 25cm.

Spots like fingerprints

No two giraffes share the same coat pattern. The patches are as individual as a fingerprint, and researchers can identify specific animals from them over years of study.

A tongue built for thorns

A giraffe's tongue can reach around 45cm (18in) long and is dark bluish-purple, likely as protection from sunburn since it spends so much time out of the mouth, wrapping around acacia branches.

An enormous heart

Pumping blood roughly two meters up to the brain takes serious power. A giraffe's heart can weigh around 11kg (25lb) and generates nearly double the blood pressure of a human.

Ossicones, not horns

Those knob-like bumps on top of a giraffe's head are called ossicones — bone structures covered in skin, present from birth (though flattened against the skull until after delivery).

Barely any sleep

Giraffes are famously light sleepers, often resting for under 30 minutes of true sleep a day, frequently in short standing naps, staying alert to predators on the open savanna.

Conservation status

Giraffe populations have declined significantly over the past few decades due to habitat loss, fragmentation, and poaching — a trend sometimes called "silent extinction" because it's drawn far less attention than the declines of other large African mammals. The species is currently listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, with some individual subspecies considered Endangered or Critically Endangered.
