Some restaurants build a concept around a chef. This one built its hours around the weather.

At Meridian Shell, the raw bar opens the instant the marine layer rolls past the ridge and closes the moment it burns off — sometimes ten in the morning, sometimes not at all. The staff call it "fog service," and regulars have learned to check a text alert rather than a posted schedule.

## A short window, on purpose

The idea started as a joke about sourcing — oysters taste better cold, fog keeps the room cold, why fight it — and turned into the restaurant's whole identity. When the window closes, the counter flips to a warm menu instead: chowder, grilled sourdough, nothing that needs ice.

It shouldn't work as a business. It does, because nobody in this city has ever needed a reason to watch the fog roll in twice.
