When a diner closes, the sign usually goes to the scrap yard within a week. For the last decade, a small nonprofit has been racing that clock.

The group's warehouse near the rail yards holds signage pulled from shuttered bakeries, motels, and one particularly beloved bowling alley — each piece tagged with the address it used to mark. Restoration is slow: original neon benders are scarce, and matching a decades-old tube color can take months of trial glass.

## A map made of light

The long-term goal isn't a museum wall. It's a walking map, letting people find where each sign used to hang and hear the short history of the business it once lit. For a city that changes its storefronts as often as this one does, that map is starting to look like a second kind of archive — one written in light instead of paper.
